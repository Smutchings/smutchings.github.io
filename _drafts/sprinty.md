# Sprinty Design Toolkits
What started as a Design Sprint at [The Familiar](https://www.thefamiliar.tech) to understand an internal problem unearthed something much more pressing within the company, the desire to make and sell their own product. Out of this Design Sprint, a few months work, and a COVID-19 driven pivot, Sprinty Design Toolkits, the Design Sprint Remote participant pack, and Sprinty Remote Session Cards were made.

## The Result
Sprinty Design Toolkits are designed to be sent out to participants before a remote design sprint or workshop, providing them with the tools they'll need to participate effectively.

[**Design Sprint Remote**](https://sprinty.xyz/collections/our-kits-and-accessories/products/design-sprint-remote-beta) is designed to be sent out to all the participants of a Remote Design Sprint, before the workshop, to provide them with all the tools and resources they'll need to effectively take part in the ideation and voting exercises that are vital to the success of the Sprint.

[**Remote Session Cards**](https://sprinty.xyz/products/remote-session-cards) are designed to help keep the flow in an online meeting, giving participants a tool to silently alert the call facilitator(s) and host(s) to an issue. 

The full selection of Sprinty Design Toolkits (including Design Sprint Remote and our Remote Session Cards) can be found and purchased at [www.sprinty.xyz](https://www.sprinty.xyz).

## The Process
In late November 2019, The Familiar identified a few internal problems that were causing friction and confusion within the company. A new Designer (myself) had joined the team in June and our Office Manager had just returned to the business after some time off. This change meant that adjustments had to be made to how the company was going to work, to make sure that everything flowed smoothly.

Together, the team decided to have a taste of their own medicine, and run a Design Sprint to identify the problems they were facing, identify potential solutions, and work to create a prototype.

### Design Sprint
In the week before Christmas Break, the team got together in the Canterbury Office to run a 4-day Design Sprint (often called Design Sprint 2.0). The team consisted of three people, with a mix of skills in Design, Development, Product Design, Graphic Design, Project Management, Copy, etc. (Ideally, a Design Sprint team should be between 5 and 8 people).

By the end of the Monday, which was spent reviewing the status quo, the aims for the Sprint, and working to understand the problems that faced us, it became very clear that there were two strands running through the "problems" category.

The first of these was project management. As the company had returned to operating size, there had been growing pains that the current software and processes we used were unable to solve. This was causing friction within the business, as it was difficult to know what each person was working on, and this problem would grow as the company looked to move remote.

The second "problem" was a deep-down desire in the company to start creating their own products. A busy 2019 had meant that this had not been possible, and this was showing in a form of resentment. 

With two problems identified, we had to vote on which one to focus on for this Design Sprint. A quick discussion and voting exercise decided two things:

- The systems and processes problem could be better addressed in a Lightning Decision Lab or other workshop.
- The focus of the Design Sprint would be to work out a product we wanted to make, create a prototype of it, and test it with customers.

On Tuesday, our research and discussions highlighted that we wanted to create a design workshop toolkit that could be used by facilitators to deliver a consistently great experience. This was driven by our own goals to deliver more workshops and our own familiarity with the area.

Wednesday was spent creating a prototype of what would now be called Sprinty Core. It was designed to be a toolkit that facilitators could order when their workshop is booked and have sent to the client. Then, when they got there, all the resources they'd need to run that Sprint or workshop would already be there.

Thursday involved testing with potential real customers, who loved the idea of Sprinty and what it could offer to them. 

### Design and Development
Now the Design Sprint had helped the team identify and prototype a product to pursue, it was time to start developing the idea out to a full product. Research and design began on Sprinty Core, its contents, packaging, pricing, delivery methods and more. The process was going well, and a launch date was set for April 2020, and then the whole world changed.

In March 2020, the COVID-19 lockdown came to Britain. This meant that businesses were no longer working from their offices, in-person workshops were no longer being run, and a product like Sprinty Core would have limited appeal or demand until the lockdown was eased. 

Whilst this change was very disheartening, the decision was made to pivot the product into a leaner, more targetted product - now called Design Sprint Remote - that could be ordered by facilitators and sent to their workshop participants to ensure they had all the tools and worksheets they need for an effective Remote Design Sprint.

In a couple of months, working around existing client obligations, the new product was designed, branded, sourced, and ready for launch.

### Launch
The launch of Sprinty Design Sprint Remote happened in July 2020, in a beta form. This phased launch allowed The Familiar to further test the waters and work out how they may need to adapt the product further. It also served as a limited-time price deal for those who had helped create Sprinty along the way. 

The full launch of Sprinty Design Sprint Remote and Remote Sessions Cards was scheduled for August 2020.
