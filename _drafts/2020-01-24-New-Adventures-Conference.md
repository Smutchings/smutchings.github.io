If there are two words that have stuck with me since my trip to New Adventures 2020 in Nottingham earlier this week, those words are "privilege" and "power". These aren't necessarily the main themes you'd expect from a Design conference, but they are, in my opinion, the very words we should be focussing on as a community and trade at the moment, and I'm glad that New Adventures was brave enough to do so.

## Privilege

To be a Designer is to be in a position of immense privilege.

Many of us in our positions or career trajectories that we enjoy. We get support from our peers and teams to push self development and try new things. We're often better compensated than the national average. And we're supported with access to some of the latest technologies on the planet and budgets for training and conferences. Especially in the world of tech, we're surrounded by options and possibilities for developing our careers or controlling our personal lives.

## Power

To be a Designer is to be in a position of immense power. You're creating the future, even if just a little part of it, and that future is something that other people must live in. Decisions made today have consequences going forward, both intentional and unintentional.

## Responsibility

It was a wise, fictional man who sad:

> With great power comes great responsibility.

With the privilege and power that comes from being a Designer, we should be more aware of our responsibilities. Responsibilities we've often just shrugged off in the past, which we just can't do anymore.

Focussing on the world of digital/tech design, as Designers, we have a responsibility to:

- Ensure that the work we do is accessible to everyone
- Ensure that work we do is environmentally sustainable
- Ensure that the work we do is financially sustainable
- Ensure that the work we do does not cause harm to other human beings or the planet
- Ensure that the work we do respects human rights, including the right to privacy, at all times
- Ensure that the work we do breaks down existing borders, walls and power structures, rather than perpetuating them
